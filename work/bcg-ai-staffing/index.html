Turning a 2-hour staffing process into a guided, 8-minute conversation

Boston Consulting Group’s staffing teams were running an open-ended, error-prone workflow. I led the design of an AI-powered tool that guides them through it step by step, so the work is faster and right the first time.

Role
Design lead: strategy, interaction design, research

01

The problem

Staffing tasks were open-ended. Each one took over two hours, and nearly a third had mistakes that someone had to catch and fix later.

How might we turn an open-ended staffing task into a guided flow that gets it right the first time?
02

Research

I led qualitative and quantitative research across 8 global staffing hubs: stakeholder interviews, data analysis and usability testing. The goal was to check assumptions with evidence before they turned into design decisions.

03

Approach

Rather than redesigning screens on top of the old process, I used systems thinking to rework the workflow logic first, removing steps that existed only because of the old tooling.

On that cleaner logic, I designed guided, conversational GenAI-style interactions. The tool asks for one decision at a time, so people are not left facing an empty form.

04

Key decisions

  • Guided steps instead of open formsA conversational sequence breaks a complex task into small, clear decisions.
  • Real-time validationErrors are caught while the user works, not after the task is submitted. This took user error from 30% to 0%.
  • UX governanceShared rules and patterns keep the experience consistent as the tool grows.
05

Working with the team

I worked closely with product managers and engineers throughout, turning research into high-fidelity Figma prototypes that were ready to build with little back-and-forth.

06

Impact

Time on task fell from 137 minutes to 8.5, a 94% reduction. User error went from 30% to 0%, and every task is now completed right the first time.

07

Reflection

Fixing the logic before the interface is what made the speed gain possible. A cleaner screen on the old process would not have changed the numbers.
